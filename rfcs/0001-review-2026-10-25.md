# Review of RFC 0001, 2026-10-25

Method: 12 lenses produced 89 raw findings, 87 unique after merge. 80 are confirmed below, 5 blocker, 47 major and 28 minor, blockers and majors by at least two of three skeptics and minors by one combined skeptic; 7 were refuted and none are left unverified. F01 to F11 were verified by the same three skeptics in an earlier pass of the run, after a network outage lost the later one.

## Decisions this review forces

1. **Decide which key signs what, and give deletion a memory.** [F01, F02, F09, G19] The member signing key sits on every device, without a passphrase on a Pi, and signs certificates, revocations, roots and delete tokens that servers honour at once, so a compromised device revokes the real devices and purges every object older than 7 days, the revocation record has no publication path, and a sibling device's cleanup deletes what a slow first backup is still uploading. Keep the member key for join, certify and revoke, with a seed-derived revoke key that never rests on a device, and sign roots and delete tokens with the device key; make deletion two-phase with a tombstone any device can clear, keep root history per device, and define the revocation record and its push path. State the cleanup invariant and let servers measure the 7-day immunity from their own last-touched time, with an in-progress marker cleanup must observe.

2. **Give roots a quorum and cleanup a coverage threshold.** [F03] PutRoot is confirmed at k acknowledgements and GetRoots reads "several" servers, so a device's newest root can sit on servers that are down when another device runs cleanup, and a new laptop can present an older snapshot as latest. State R, W and S with W a majority of listed servers, refuse cleanup below S minus W plus 1 answers, keep root history on servers, remember the last published version per device, and show "roots for X of Y devices from Z of S servers" at restore.

3. **Never delete on absence from the manifest.** [F04] The orphan sweep deletes an unlisted owner's shares within one sync, grace_days has no start date, and a key edited in place reads as removal plus a stranger. Servers record first_seen_missing and delete only when their own clock shows the grace elapsed and the manifest still lacks or marks the member; the schema gains leaving_since, validate refuses an early second merge and treats a member's key as immutable, and Identity says in one sentence that there is no key rotation.

4. **Settle the trust roots: release key rotation and the hub mirror.** [F06, F07] The release key rotates through a manifest that is unsigned until phase 4, so a repository account can auto-install a binary on every device, and mirror users take membership, revocations and the release index from a hub Principle 3 calls untrusted. Rotate only through a chain signed by the current release key or through the signed manifest, let min_version select among chain-signed releases, and either drop the mirror until signing exists or move one-key manifest signing into phase 1 with revision, issued_at and max_age reserved in the envelope. Rewrite the repository, pipeline and hub threat rows to what the design delivers.

5. **Bind the encoding into the object identity and give a share states.** [F18, F17, F19, G14, G09] The storage index depends only on the key, so a re-encode collides with the old shares, dedup skips the re-upload and the delete token cannot separate the two encodings. Fold k, n and segment size into the key as Tahoe does, or address shares by storage index plus descriptor hash everywhere; give a share two states, partial and complete; make the descriptor canonical; carry k, n and descriptor hash in the census tuple; state that the bytes hashed for the key are the bytes encrypted.

6. **Say what servers really enforce, and tie budgets to the grid.** [F16, G21, G23, G20, F20] With headroom 2 the server bound is twice the budget and the exact check stays on the member's own client; five passages say otherwise. Pick the tight fix or the honest wording, state that allocation takes the declared contribution as input, bound the per-server cap by the server's own contribution, let `redundanet validate` refuse a reserve below the largest server's share of the grid, and define the accounted size of a share with an object-count ceiling.

7. **Give non-owner writes a proof of need and give deletion a memory.** [F15, G19, F21, G27, G22, G24] Nothing caps how many servers hold a share of one object, nothing verifies under-placement before a repair write is accepted, nothing tombstones a deleted object, and nothing says when a server is dead rather than down. Add the cap at n, a repair task token or signed HasShares evidence, DeleteShare, tombstones reported in the census, a down versus dead window, a quiescence window before a repair task, a collector target of min(n, S_live), and two traffic classes so repair and serving have a stated share of the uplink.

8. **Fix the membership surface before the hub holds a write token.** [F11, F12, G05, F13, F14, F40] Either keep PR creation in GitHub Actions with the hub opening an issue, or specify the bot token's scope, stale-approval dismissal and a CI-verified applicant signature, and say that the inviter compares the 10-group code out of band. The join endpoint is anonymous and vouched_by is a typed name, so make the vouch a signed invite the hub verifies against the manifest before touching GitHub, and give the schema sponsor and legacy_pgp records. Resolve `leaving` into one state table, make a storage device a manifest entry with F2 rewritten for client devices only, give the join endpoint a limiter key and a cost, and decide the fork's update channel.

9. **Make phase 1 restore from the phrase true, or move it.** [F23, F22, F24, G03] On the Tahoe adapter nothing lets a new device find a member's roots from twelve words, so F15 and the new-laptop journey are unmet until the native engine unless a root registry is specified. Split F16 into an unattended test from stored keys and an opt-in paper check, restrict phrase prompts to loopback, and decide whether existing members keep their Tahoe convergence secret so the first phase 1 backup is not a full re-upload.

10. **Write the backup job's filesystem contract.** [G09, G10, G11, G12, G15, G16, G17] The scan has no ctime, no per-file failure rule, no file-kind table, no placeholder policy, no partial snapshot and no restore write discipline. Each is one paragraph, and together they fix the directory entry format, which is versioned from day one and should be settled before Accepted.

11. **Rewrite the migration plan with a step 0 and gates the maintainer controls.** [F05, F08, F25, F26, F27, F28, F38, F39] Hubs and identity bootstrap come before any sidecar; step 3 is a scripted privileged migration per node with a real rollback; phase 3 ends on a straggler rule rather than on the slowest member; G1 splits into the first cross-platform member and tinc gone everywhere. A transitional manifest carries the v2 keys beside the v3 ones, with both validators in CI until the v2 half is dropped after step 3, and step 3 imports each node's tahoe-client aliases as legacy roots that the phase 1 lease sweep walks before the compose stack goes.

12. **Price the transport honestly and close the platform gaps the design rests on.** [F36, F35, F37, F10, F31, F32, F33, F34, F30, G01, G02] Seamless relay-to-direct switching is a layer below QUIC that the Go column prices at a few hundred lines; size it or relax the Transfers NFR. Decide a TCP 443 relay fallback, measure AES-GCM on the Pi 4, give self-update a supervisor, scope Goal 1 for storage on Windows and macOS, add TCC handling and a storage-unavailable state, pair the Tahoe bundle with the agent version, and put CSRF, Host and Origin checks and a TLS-or-no-LAN decision on the local page. A "Wire identity" paragraph says how the device certificate rides in the TLS 1.3 handshake and what both verifiers check.

## Blockers

### F01. The member signing key on every device makes revocation protect nothing
- Where: Identity and keys, key table, Losing a device and Key storage at rest; Security model; Health, Deletion.
- The RFC says: "A stolen laptop without the phrase can read what it had cached and nothing new after that." against "Every device of the member, encrypted at rest".
- What is wrong: The member signing key certifies devices and signs revocations, root records and delete tokens, and it sits on every device: a headless Pi holds it without a passphrase, and on a desktop the OS keystore releases it to the logged-in session, so whoever holds an unlocked or malware-compromised laptop, or any reachable Pi, holds the member key. That holder mints a fresh device certificate, so revoking the stolen one is a no-op, revokes the real devices, publishes a root with a huge version so they can never publish again, and signs delete tokens for every object older than 7 days, which every server honours at once with no tombstone. The stolen-device row's "Act after revocation" under Cannot and the quoted sentence claim the opposite, and the sentence is wrong for reads too, since the device holds the member secret that decrypts every root record of the member, not only its cache. Today a compromised node can overwrite the root directory it holds caps for, but immutable shares persist for the 90-day lease and nothing lets it purge shares on other servers; the RFC removes that cushion. Four lenses raised it.
- Why it matters: A compromised device destroys a member's backups everywhere within minutes with valid signatures, and the offered remedy revokes a certificate the attacker no longer needs.
- Proposed change: Derive the member key from the phrase only for member-level acts, join, certify and revoke, and zeroize it; sign roots, descriptors and delete tokens with the device key under its certificate, carrying not_before and expiry so earlier uploads and repair stay valid, and state the trade-off that revocation then needs the phrase in hand. Make deletion two-phase, tombstone for 30 days then purge, with any device able to un-delete, and keep the last N root versions per device; the tombstone, which G19 also asks for, is the load-bearing mitigation, because a device-signed token still gives an attacker a full sync interval before revocation lands. Rewrite "Losing a device" and the stolen-device row. If the key must stay on devices, say that a stolen unlocked device equals a stolen phrase, require a passphrase or keystore everywhere, and drop the revocation claim.

### F02. Deletion without leases has no coordination between a member's devices
- Where: Backup and restore, Scan, Publish and Retention; Health, Deletion; Users and jobs to be done.
- The RFC says: "objects younger than 7 days are never deleted, so a device that is mid-backup cannot have its fresh objects removed by another device of the same member running cleanup".
- What is wrong: An object lives while a published root references it, cleanup on any device of the member deletes the rest, roots are published only at the end of a run, and the only guard is the 7-day age rule. On the NFR's 0.5 Mbit/s uplink a first backup above about 19 GB of files outlives that window before PutRoot, and a laptop closed for ten days mid-run resumes from its queue with no re-verification, so in both cases the published root points at objects a sibling device's cleanup has already deleted and every later scan is a cache hit on the dangling capability. An object whose only snapshot was pruned is by construction older than 7 days, so a HasShares hit on device B reuses it without upload while device A's cleanup deletes it, and a plain race between cleanup's GetRoots and its delete tokens does the same even when every device behaves. restic takes an exclusive lock to prune and today's sweep relies on Tahoe's add-lease during a backup; the RFC drops both without a replacement. Three lenses raised it.
- Why it matters: Silent data loss for the RFC's own model of a member, a Pi plus a laptop, discovered at restore in the main feature; Goal 8 is false and the risk row rated "Low with care" does not cover it.
- Proposed change: State the invariant under Deletion: nothing unreferenced is deleted while any device of the member has uploaded or claimed it inside the window or can still publish a root referencing it. Pick one mechanism: the server, not the owner, is the clock, every PutShare and every HasShares hit from an authenticated device of the owner bumps a server-side last_touched_at from which the 7-day immunity is measured, and cleanup must observe an in-progress marker, a root version with a pending flag or a short-lived cleanup lock record on servers; or each device publishes its pending set of storage indices every few minutes during a run and cleanup treats it as reachable. Before PutRoot, verify with HasShares every capability not in the device's previous root. Tombstone then purge, as under F01, is a useful belt but not required for the invariant.

### F03. Root write quorum and read set need not intersect, and client devices are unlisted
- Where: Mutable state, The root record; Health, Deletion; Membership; NFR Consistency.
- The RFC says: "A write is confirmed when at least k servers acknowledge it, and the job retries the others." against "`GetRoots` asks several servers".
- What is wrong: PutRoot fans out to every live server but is confirmed at k acknowledgements, the erasure parameter, while GetRoots reads an unspecified "several" with no stated overlap, no rule that R plus W exceeds S, and no bound on retries, which stall whenever the writing laptop goes offline. Client devices are "never listed", so cleanup cannot know that a device exists whose root it did not find, and the Deletion claim that a lost local database "cannot cause a wrong deletion" rests on a complete root view the design never ensures. The window is narrower than a first draft would say: the per-backup republish and the 7-day rule mean a wrong deletion needs the acknowledging servers down, the retries incomplete and more than 7 days elapsed, and a whole backup set is exposed only for a device whose sole root version never left the k acknowledging servers. The stale-version case survives intact: device B's newest root sits on servers that are down when device A runs cleanup, so B's objects older than 7 days that only that newer index references are deleted, and a phrase-only restore on a new laptop has no reference version and silently presents an older snapshot as latest. Multi-day server absence is ordinary here, and the RFC itself says a usage report 11 days old is one member's normal state; two lenses raised it, and the domain skeptic would rate it major because the fix is bounded to one section.
- Why it matters: Deletion is irrevocable, one laptop's cleanup can silently delete another laptop's backups, and a new device can present a rolled-back root as the latest; Goal 3 and Principle 2 rest on neither happening.
- Proposed change: State R, W and S: PutRoot confirmed by a majority W of listed storage servers, GetRoots querying all and reporting how many answered, cleanup refused and restore warning unless at least S minus W plus 1 answered, which guarantees every confirmed root is seen, while an unconfirmed root belongs to a backup that never completed and is covered by the 7-day rule. Servers keep root history and each device remembers its last published version. The restore screen shows "roots for X of Y devices from Z of S servers". A signed list of the certificates a member issued, mirrored beside the revocation list, lets cleanup refuse while any non-revoked device's root is unknown; it is useful rather than required once W is defined.

### F04. The orphan sweep and grace "from the manifest alone" delete data on a slip
- Where: Health, Deletion; Membership, schema and Leaving and removal; Ownership, Leaving.
- The RFC says: "Servers delete all shares and roots of a member whose grace period has ended, from the manifest alone, and run an orphan sweep for owners no longer listed."
- What is wrong: A server deletes every share and root of an owner absent from the manifest with no grace at all, and a dropped list item, a revert or an early second merge is indistinguishable from the post-grace removal, so a broken manifest on main, which reaches live nodes within five minutes and today cuts VPN peers, here deletes shares on every server within one sync. grace_days is network-wide and no entry carries a date, so "from the manifest alone" cannot be computed: each server observes the flip to leaving at a different time, a long-offline server late, and validate cannot refuse a second merge made before the grace has run. The RFC deliberately has no member-key rotation, a new phrase being a new identity, so the key case is narrower than a first draft would say: nothing forbids editing key in place on an existing entry, which the sweep treats as removal plus a stranger, and the RFC never says who re-uploads after an identity change. Today shares survive 90 days without lease renewal, a de facto grace the design removes; the sweep lands with the native engine in phase 3, which changes when the risk bites, not whether.
- Why it matters: Irreversible loss of a member's whole backup from a YAML slip or a routine revert, executed autonomously by every server; Goal 8 is contradicted and the risk table counts engine bugs as the only data-loss path.
- Proposed change: Tombstone semantics: a server never deletes on absence; it records owner and first_seen_missing and deletes only when its own clock shows grace_days elapsed and the current manifest still lacks or marks the member. Add leaving_since to the schema; validate refuses leaving without it and refuses removing an entry that has not been leaving for grace_days. Make the key of an existing member entry immutable in validate: a new key is a new entry plus the old one marked leaving, which puts the old data under the normal grace. Say in Identity and keys, in one sentence, that there is no member-key rotation.

### F05. Step 3 orphans every existing Tahoe backup
- Where: Storage engine interface and the Tahoe adapter; Migration plan, steps 3 and 4; Phases; Goal 8.
- The RFC says: "old Tahoe snapshots stay readable through the adapter" against "removes the compose stack".
- What is wrong: Members' data lives under tahoe backup aliases in the tahoe-client volume, kept alive only by the lease sweep in that container, while every server runs the 90-day expiry the adapter keeps in the tahoe.cfg it writes, and the patched crawler has resumed collection since 2026-10-17. The only root model the RFC defines is the per-device mutable directory the new backup job writes: the words alias and backupdb occur nowhere, nothing imports the alias caps, the restore browser lists roots only, and the "lease sweep" the Phases list names for phase 1 has no stated scope, so legacy aliases fall outside it. Step 3 removes the compose stack, which is the tahoe-client and its sweep, and reuses only the server's node directory, so step 4's claim holds for nothing backed up before the agent existed. The loss is bounded by the agent's first phase 1 backup: unchanged files re-uploaded from their sources under the same convergence secret get fresh leases, so what expires is the historical Archives snapshots, the alias directories and every file since deleted at the source, up to 263k objects; the convergence mismatch that would widen it is F24. The risk is prospective, since the first completed cycles on 2026-10-18 deleted nothing; two lenses raised it, and the domain skeptic would rate it major because the import is a short addition.
- Why it matters: Silent loss of pre-migration history and deleted-at-source files about 90 days after each node's step 3, with nothing on the status page warning; "without losing a file" and the per-node rollback claim are false for them.
- Proposed change: Make step 3 an import: on first start on a node migrated from a compose stack, the Tahoe adapter seeds the device's root from the alias dircaps in the client's private/aliases, which are already mutable directories, the RFC's own root shape, and records them encrypted so the phrase still finds them. State that the phase 1 lease sweep and the restore browser walk legacy roots like snapshots until step 5. Keep or import the tahoe-client node directory before the stack is removed. Add to G1 that every retired client's aliases are referenced by a device root; refusing to remove the stack until a check of every legacy root passes is optional hardening.

## Major

### F06. The release key rotates through the unsigned manifest
- Where: Packaging; Self-update; Membership, Distribution; Security model; Principle 9.
- The RFC says: "a release key whose public half is embedded in the binary and can be rotated through the manifest".
- What is wrong: The manifest is unsigned until phase 4 and no rotation rule is specified, so whoever can merge it names a new release key, raises min_version and has every agent auto-install their binary within the hour with the member key decrypted in memory; the compromised-repository row's "Read data" under Cannot is false outright, and the pipeline row's "Have it installed" is false whenever the pipeline can land a manifest change. Not a regression, since today CI builds unsigned :latest images from main that the fleet auto-pulls: the defect is that the RFC presents the offline release key as a security upgrade and reopens the hole with one clause. Two lenses raised it; the materiality skeptic lowered it from blocker because the fix is small and the exposure already exists today.
- Why it matters: For the eight to eleven months before manifest signing, and on every fork following the index, one GitHub credential is code execution on every member device and plaintext access to every backup, against Goal 6 and Principle 3.
- Proposed change: Rotate only through the release chain, a new key and valid_from signed by the current key in the signed index with a grace window, or through the phase 4 signed manifest, with min_version selecting only among chain-signed releases; or move one-key manifest signing into phase 1. Rewrite both threat rows to say a repository account controls membership but not the binary until phase 4, and give forks a release index and keys entry with the rule that a fork trusts upstream's chain or signs its own binary. See F40.

### F07. The hub's mirror is trusted while the hub is declared untrusted
- Where: Membership, Distribution; Transport, Who may connect; Hub, Directory row; Principle 3; Security model.
- The RFC says: "with the hub as a mirror for agents that cannot reach GitHub" against "a hub fully compromised can delay traffic and lie on a web page, nothing else".
- What is wrong: An agent on the mirror computes its allowlist from a manifest the hub serves unsigned until phase 4, with no revision, issued_at or max age ever, so a compromised hub adds a member key and mirror users, not the whole grid, admit a device that in phase 1 reaches the introducer and their Tahoe servers through the bridge with client-enforced quotas; after signing, a stale copy keeps removed members and revoked devices connected and stops a raised min_version from forcing an update, though it cannot install an older binary. Removing the mirror does not alone make the hub row true in phase 1, since a listed hub is allowlisted on the mesh and hosts the introducer, so it can write shares through the bridge until the native engine verifies ownership, and a hub can withhold a revocation, which already contradicts the stolen-device row for peers that learn revocations only from the hub. The materiality skeptic lowered it from blocker because the mirror path serves nobody on the live network today and the hub operator is the repository owner.
- Why it matters: Principle 3, F7 and the hub table are false for the population the mirror exists for, and forks inherit the trust model Goal 7 asks them to rely on.
- Proposed change: Remove the unauthenticated mirror until manifest signing exists, or make one-key signing a phase 1 precondition for it, with the same envelope over the revocation list and the release index and with revision, issued_at and max_age reserved now, past which an agent stops accepting new connections and writes. Add "serve a stale or forged manifest to mirror users" and a phase 1 bridge-write caveat to the compromised-hub row, or state plainly that mirror users trust the hub for membership until phase 4.

### F08. The v3 manifest cannot live on main while any tinc node exists
- Where: Membership; Migration plan, steps 1 to 3; Local API, CLI and UI; Open questions.
- The RFC says: "there is no VPN" against "Old clients still reach servers over tinc; nothing else changes."
- What is wrong: Schema 3 drops nodes, VPN addresses, introducer furls and the network fields while steps 1 to 3 keep tinc and the Tahoe containers on every node reading the same raw URL every five minutes, and the RFC never says when the manifest switches, what the old Python readers see meanwhile or how a revert works, though step 1 itself needs a loopback port per storage device. As the code stands CI refuses the RFC's own example through validate_pr and the "No introducer configured" error; relaxed, manifest_sync hands the peer writer an empty node list within one 300-second cycle, which unlinks every peer host file and HUPs tincd on every unmigrated node, while a running Tahoe container keeps its introducer until its next restart. The binary name collision, two redundanet update commands with opposite meanings, bites only on Linux nodes during step 3 and is minor; three lenses raised it, and the materiality skeptic lowered it from blocker because the hole is in the migration plan, not the architecture, and costs availability until a revert, not data.
- Why it matters: Either step 1 cannot ship its manifest change or shipping it is the flag day Goal 8 forbids, and "Each step is reversible on its own" is unsupported.
- Proposed change: A "transitional manifest" subsection: every v2 key kept verbatim plus the v3 keys, members[].key, nodes[].device, nodes[].loopback_port and network.min_version; a 2.x validate checking both halves and failing closed, both validators in CI, the agent ignoring v2 keys and keeping its last valid manifest, rollback as a revert both readers accept, and the v2 half dropped after step 3 as its own gate. State that the Python CLI leaves with the compose stack at step 3, or decide the binary name now. See F26.

### F09. Device revocation records have no content, publication path or authority
- Where: Identity and keys, Losing a device; Hub, Directory row; Transport, Who may connect; Local API; Open questions; Security model.
- The RFC says: "a signed record the hub mirrors with the manifest and that every peer and server honours within one sync interval".
- What is wrong: The hub "fetches them from the repository itself", so the list lives in Git, and under the recommended answer to the open question the app holds no GitHub token, so a record reaches the repository only through the hub's bot token, a human merge of hours to days or an unreviewed bot push that lets a pushing hub revoke every device; if the hub serves revocations directly instead, a compromised hub suppresses them and GitHub readers never see them. The quoted sentence holds once a record is published; never written down are who publishes it and how, what it contains beyond the serial the certificate already carries, how caching peers and servers learn of it and persist it, and the wire protocol behind the revoke action of /devices. The fix must also face F01: a revocation signed by the member key can be forged by whoever holds an unlocked or passphrase-less device, who can also certify a fresh device key, so a per-device revocation alone is sidestepped; four lenses raised it.
- Why it matters: A stolen device keeps access, and with F01 deletion power, until a maintainer merges or while an attacker jams the hub; revocation is either slow and human-gated or hands the hub an unreviewed write path.
- Proposed change: Define the record, member, device public key, certificate serial, revoked_at and a per-member monotonic seq, signed by a seed-derived revoke key never persisted on devices, or state plainly that losing an unlocked device means a new member identity, as the stolen-phrase row does. The revoking device pushes it over the mesh to every reachable server and hub, with an unauthenticated HTTPS /revoke on the hub as the fallback; servers persist it, close open connections and refuse later certificates of that member unless the revoke key signed them; hubs serve the union, the repository copy is archival, acceptors re-check on every handshake, and "within one sync interval" becomes "at the next connection to each server and within one census interval".

### F10. The device certificate's wire form in the TLS 1.3 handshake is unspecified
- Where: Transport, Who may connect; Security model, Primitives; Spike plan.
- The RFC says: "the device key as the TLS identity" against "The handshake carries the device certificate".
- What is wrong: TLS 1.3 in Go carries X.509 chains, not raw Ed25519 keys, and the RFC never says how the device certificate travels in the handshake, whether both sides present one or what each verifier checks, so "refused before any application byte" rests on an undefined binding. The text is underspecified rather than self-contradictory, since the certificate is never said to be CBOR and its fields map onto an X.509 leaf issued by the member signing key; the hub exception is stated at the policy level and only its wire form is missing, and 0-RTT is off by default in quic-go. The materiality skeptic refuted it as having no design consequence, since the same device key is pinned by TLS and the same member binding is checked before any storage action; it survived two to one.
- Why it matters: F7 and every allowlist claim rest on the binding, and a custom verifier in Go, InsecureSkipVerify plus VerifyPeerCertificate, is a known footgun better designed than discovered.
- Proposed change: A short "Wire identity" paragraph that picks one encoding, an X.509 leaf whose subject key is the device key and whose issuer is the member signing key, or a self-signed leaf carrying the member-signed binding in a critical extension under a project OID as libp2p-tls does; client certificates on every connection; both sides verifying leaf key, member signature, active status, revocation list and the listed-hub case; 0-RTT disabled. Let the spike confirm the encoding if the library choice changes it.

### F11. The join endpoint is anonymous and the vouch is a self-asserted name
- Where: Hub, Join endpoint; Membership, Joining and schema; Principle 5; Identity and keys, Display; Ownership, Client-only members; Security model.
- The RFC says: "The inviter is asked in the pull request to confirm, which is the social step." against "Test: the manifest says who vouched for whom."
- What is wrong: Today a join is a GitHub issue, so the requester has a login and GitHub's anti-abuse sits in front of the bot; the RFC replaces it with a hub endpoint anyone can post to, signed by a fresh key and naming an inviter by a public name, with an unqualified "rate-limits", and the threat table's "per-device rate limit" cannot apply to an applicant whose key is not yet a device, while the external poster is absent from the adversary table. A spoofed request cannot admit anyone, because the inviter confirms in the PR before merge, so the consequence is PR noise, bot-token exposure and operator fatigue rather than false admission; the narrower defects are that vouched_by carries no proof and that confirming needs the inviter to hold a GitHub account, which a member who "never saw Git" or an inviter in a fork without GitHub logins does not. "Vouch" also names the applicant's PGP self-signature and a sponsor's allocation to a client-only member, for which the schema has neither allocation_gb nor a sponsor field; two lenses raised it.
- Why it matters: The hub's bot token is the only thing between an anonymous poster and the repository's PR list, the vouch graph Principle 5 tests is a list of typed names, and client-only allocations cannot be expressed or enforced.
- Proposed change: Make the vouch a signed invite: the inviter's app signs network, nonce, expiry and an optional applicant name or allocation and hands it over out of band; the hub verifies it against the manifest before touching GitHub and refuses anything else; the PR embeds invite and applicant signature, CI verifies both, and vouched_by records member plus invite signature, with the issue form kept as a fallback. Add legacy_pgp with fingerprint and signature, and sponsor with member and gigabytes, with a validate rule that allocations times n over k fit contribution times one minus reserve. See G05 and F12.


### F12. The bot token contradicts "the hub holds no key but its own"
- Where: Membership, Joining; Hub, State; Security model; Principle 3.
- The RFC says: "the hub holds no key but its own".
- What is wrong: A long-lived bot token, admitted under State and denied in the threat table, lets a compromised hub push a key swap onto a confirmed join PR or substitute the member key before the inviter sees it; branch protection keeps it off main. Two lenses raised it.
- Why it matters: The hub's compromise shapes what the maintainer merges.
- Proposed change: Prefer an issues-only token with today's Actions workflow making the PR, else a fine-grained token with stale-approval dismissal and CODEOWNERS. CI verifies the applicant's signature in the PR, the inviter compares the 10-group code out of band, and the hub row and principle 3 list the token. See G05.

### F13. `leaving` is defined four ways
- Where: Membership; Ownership, Leaving; Transport; journey "I leave".
- The RFC says: "keeps read access during the grace period" against "their devices are refused on connect".
- What is wrong: Three passages refuse a leaving member at connect; Ownership grants reads during grace, which servers cannot implement if the transport never admits the device; in phase 1 grace_days is unenforceable anyway. Four lenses raised it.
- Why it matters: One section is false whichever reading wins.
- Proposed change: One state table in Membership: leaving connects and reads, writes and repair-on-behalf refused; removed refused at the handshake, shares deleted grace_days after the leaving merge; allowlist "active or leaving".

### F14. F2 promises storage devices without a manifest change the schema cannot give
- Where: F2; Membership schema; journey "My node dies".
- The RFC says: "without a change to the manifest, with a role per device".
- What is wrong: Storage devices are manifest entries by device key, name and port, counted by quotas and placement, yet the join request carries no device key and a replaced Pi is unaddressed. Three lenses raised it.
- Why it matters: A Must requirement the design cannot implement.
- Proposed change: Rewrite F2 for client devices only; add the first storage device key, name and port to the join request; add `device add --storage`, a member-signed entry that opens a one-line PR; extend "My node dies" to a replaced Pi.

### F15. Non-owner writes have no bound and no proof of need
- Where: Ownership, Who may store what; Health, Repair; Security model.
- The RFC says: "A device may store shares owned by another member (that is what repair is)".
- What is wrong: PutShare never checks that an object is under-placed or caps holders at n, so any device can regenerate all n shares of anyone's object and push them everywhere, charged to the victim up to headroom times budget; two hubs or a stale worker do the same by accident. Four lenses raised it.
- Why it matters: Two threat rows deny this; surplus has no reclaim path.
- Proposed change: One share per storage index per server; non-owner PutShare only with a hub-signed repair token or signed HasShares results showing fewer than n holders; cap at n; add DeleteShare; correct the storage-host and malicious-member rows. See G19.

### F16. Server enforcement is a 2x bound; the exact check is the client's
- Where: Ownership, Enforcement per server; F10; Principle 8; Summary; Security model.
- The RFC says: "budget / S × headroom" and "The global check is the client's".
- What is wrong: Caps sum to twice the budget, so a member who patches the client check out of their GPL binary stores 2x before any server refuses, while five passages say servers enforce the allocation.
- Why it matters: The phase 2 headline is half true.
- Proposed change: Either servers receive each owner's grid-wide usage as a signed roll-up with each census acknowledgement and refuse above budget, or the five passages say the server bound is headroom times budget and the client holds the exact one.

### F17. HasShares "present" is undefined; partial shares look complete
- Where: Backup, Chunking and Scan; Ownership; Native engine, Share on disk.
- The RFC says: "`PutShare` resumes by offset for a share the server already holds partially" against "the share is not already present".
- What is wrong: The index has no completeness column, so HasShares, the census and the not-already-present rule count partial shares: device A crashes mid-share, device B is told present, skips the upload and publishes a snapshot with no complete share. The same rule refuses the resume Chunking promises.
- Why it matters: Files appear stored while no recoverable copy exists.
- Proposed change: A share state machine: partial, invisible to HasShares and census, expiring if not completed; complete, verified against the signed root at close. Present means at least k complete shares; PutShare refuses a complete duplicate and resumes a partial one. See G27.

### F18. The storage index does not bind the encoding
- Where: Native engine, Key and storage index, Descriptor; Health, Rebalance.
- The RFC says: "`storage_index = BLAKE3("si" || key)[0:16]`".
- What is wrong: The key ignores k, n and segment size, so a re-encode at 3-of-6 lands on the 2-of-4 storage index: "not already present" refuses the new shares, HasShares never re-uploads, the census is blind to encoding, and the delete token cannot retire one encoding alone. Three lenses raised it.
- Why it matters: F19 cannot be met.
- Proposed change: Fold k, n and segment size into the key as Tahoe does, or address shares by storage index plus descriptor hash everywhere. Make the descriptor canonical: `created` leaves the signed record, servers refuse a differing descriptor hash, HasShares returns the held one.

### F19. The census tuple carries no encoding parameters
- Where: Health, Census and Rebalance.
- The RFC says: "objects with the old encoding are listed by the hub from their descriptors".
- What is wrong: The census tuple carries no k, n or descriptor hash and the descriptor reaches the hub by no described path, so it can neither judge under-placement against each object's own n nor build the rebalance list.
- Why it matters: After any change that lowers n, real under-placement is hidden.
- Proposed change: Add k, n and descriptor_hash to the SQLite index and the census tuple; the hub computes placement per object against that object's n.

### F20. The accounting unit is undefined and per-object overhead is uncharged
- Where: Ownership, The budget; Native engine, Share on disk; NFR Footprint.
- The RFC says: "A member's grid budget is allocation × n/k bytes of shares".
- What is wrong: Usage is the index's `size`, never defined, and nothing charges the n share files, headers, hash lists, rounding and inodes an object costs: one million small files make 4M share files, and 10M files of 257 bytes put 5M index rows per server against a 1M-share NFR.
- Why it matters: One home directory exhausts a server's inodes for a few gigabytes of quota.
- Proposed change: Define accounted size as block plus header and hash bytes rounded to a per-share minimum, plus a per-member object-count ceiling; a pack object would break per-object convergent dedup.

### F21. "Declared dead" is undefined
- Where: NFR Durability; Health, Repair; journey "My node dies"; Hub, Two hubs.
- The RFC says: "repair starts within one hour of a server being declared dead".
- What is wrong: Nothing defines dead or says whether an offline server's shares still count, as today's monitor assumes; a multi-hour home outage then triggers re-placement and leaves n+1 shares charged to the owner with no release path. Two hubs may both schedule. Two lenses raised it.
- Why it matters: Repair churn from ordinary outages, or a promise the system cannot keep.
- Proposed change: Define down, shares still counting, and dead, past a manifest window such as repair_after_hours; repair acts only on dead servers and integrity failures; shares above n verified are hub-listed and releasable without the owner; one scheduler across hubs. See G27.

### F22. F16's restore test cannot run unattended as designed
- Where: F16; Identity, Losing the phrase; Backup, Restore test; Success metrics.
- The RFC says: "runs the derivation from the words the member types, not from the stored keys".
- What is wrong: F16 and the metric say the app runs the test on its own; Identity and Restore test start it from the typed phrase. A headless Pi has nobody to type, and a monthly prompt trains members to type their phrase into dialogs. Two lenses raised it.
- Why it matters: Requirement, design and metric describe different features.
- Proposed change: F16a, an unattended sample restore from stored keys on any device; F16b, an opt-in "check my recovery phrase" action, never scheduled; align the metric to F16b and name what "compares hashes" compares. See G03.

### F23. Phase 1 has no way from the phrase to a member's roots
- Where: Tahoe adapter; Backup, Restore; Hub, State; Success metrics; Phases.
- The RFC says: "GetRoots(ctx, member Key) ([]Root, error)".
- What is wrong: Tahoe cannot enumerate a member's directories and a mkdir directory gets a random RSA key, so a device holding only the seed cannot find per-device roots, and the RFC names no registry. Phase 2 lists roots as native work while the metrics demand phrase-only restores after phase 1. Two lenses raised it.
- Why it matters: F15 and the "Restore on a new laptop" journey are unmet in phase 1.
- Proposed change: Specify a phase 1 root registry: a member root directory via t=mkdir&private-key= from a pinned deterministic RSA derivation, an encrypted root cap in the manifest entry or on every server, or hub-mirrored encrypted pointers. Or move new-device restore to phase 2.

### F24. Phase 1's first backup is a full re-upload under enforced quotas
- Where: Tahoe adapter; Identity, key table; Migration plan, step 4.
- The RFC says: "Member secret ... Derives the convergence secret".
- What is wrong: Each tahoe-client has a random convergence secret; deriving it from the seed yields a different CHK for every file already on the grid, so data sits twice for up to 90 days and the client check pauses the backup above allocation. Step 4 exempts only the phase 2 re-upload. Two lenses raised it.
- Why it matters: Every existing member experiences phase 1 as "my backup stopped".
- Proposed change: Let an existing node adopt the tahoe-client's `private/convergence` as the member's secret, stored encrypted in the root record, with only new members deriving from the seed; or state the re-upload in Phases with the step 4 accounting rule. Correct the key table.

### F25. Step 3's "tub keys travel" skips the real obstacles
- Where: Migration plan, step 3; NFR Privileges.
- The RFC says: "Rollback per node: the compose stack is still on disk until the member deletes it".
- What is wrong: The Tahoe node directory is a root-owned named volume and the storage disk a root-owned bind mount, while the agent runs as an ordinary user; moving them and rewriting tahoe.cfg for host paths is a privileged, unscripted operation per node, and rollback must restore tahoe.cfg, which the compose entrypoint never regenerates. Two lenses raised it.
- Why it matters: Each migration is a manual root-level operation on nodes the maintainer cannot reach.
- Proposed change: Write step 3 as `redundanet migrate-node`: stop the stack, chown in place, rewrite tahoe.cfg keeping a backup, verify marker and tub identity, start the agent, confirm the same server id on the page; rollback is the reverse; say the step is privileged.

### F26. Step 1 preconditions are unstated
- Where: Migration plan, steps 1 and 2; Identity and keys.
- The RFC says: "nothing else changes".
- What is wrong: Every existing member must first generate a seed, get device certificates onto headless nodes and submit members[].key and storage[].device; nothing schedules this or attests that the new key belongs to today's GPG key holder. Step 1 is itself a 2.x image release, so step 2 waits on the slowest operator. Two lenses raised it.
- Why it matters: Step 1 stalls on six members doing an undocumented identity ceremony on headless machines.
- Proposed change: Add "Step 0: identity bootstrap": `redundanet identity init` prints the phrase and manifest fragment; headless nodes receive a certificate issued on the member's laptop; the PGP continuity signature is mandatory for migrating members; one PR adds members[] and storage[]; step 2 starts when every storage device announces its loopback hint.

### F27. No migration step for the hubs
- Where: Hub; Migration plan.
- The RFC says: "a second hub takes over as today".
- What is wrong: Today only the fly hub runs a collector, pulling census over VPN IPs; hub-hetzner runs none. The five steps never deploy the hub binary, say when the fly collector retires, how introducer #1 leaves fly, or what fly becomes at G1 without tinc.
- Why it matters: The status page loses nodes during step 3 and everything at G1.
- Proposed change: Step 0 "hubs first": the hub binary on hub-hetzner before any sidecar, its collector ingesting the pull census and the mesh census side by side until step 3 ends; native members use the hub-supervised introducer; the fly introducer and page get a retirement date; a hub line in the gates.

### F30. The Tahoe bundle has no specification
- Where: Packaging; Tahoe adapter; Risks; Alternatives.
- The RFC says: "downloaded by the agent on first start and verified the same way".
- What is wrong: Unstated: how the patch is applied before freezing, the glibc floor, the pairing of bundle hash with agent version, bundle rollback when self-update swaps only the binary, signing of the bundle's executables, a missing or quarantined child, a smoke test. Alternatives rejects PyInstaller for antivirus friction, yet every Windows and macOS node runs exactly that. Two lenses raised it.
- Why it matters: A bundle fix cannot ship and a rollback can leave a mismatched pair.
- Proposed change: A "Tahoe bundle" subsection: the release index pairs agent version with bundle hash, self-update swaps and rolls back both as one unit, a missing child maps to a visible storage-unavailable state, the signing line covers the bundle, all in the phase 1 scope.

### F31. Self-update rollback has no supervisor
- Where: Self-update; journey "Update".
- The RFC says: "A failed check swaps the previous binary back and restarts".
- What is wrong: If the new binary never starts, crash at init, failed data migration, quarantine, no process exists to roll back and the OS relaunches it; today `redundanet update` is separate from what it replaces. The peer-reached check also fails by construction for a fork's first member, who then swaps, fails and rolls back hourly.
- Why it matters: The most common update failure strands the node, against F20 and "stranded: 0".
- Proposed change: Two-stage update: the running binary stays as supervisor, starts the new one in probation and commits only after the checks. Say the Windows swap is rename-based, make the peer check relative to what the node reached before, record failed versions, pin the Tahoe asset.

### F32. Storage on Windows and macOS does not survive a reboot without a login
- Where: Service integration; NFR Privileges; Goal 1; Principle 7.
- The RFC says: "starts at login (and at boot on Linux with lingering)".
- What is wrong: A launchd user agent dies at logout, macOS has no non-root always-on path, and FileVault disables auto-login. On Windows a logon task survives Update restarts but not power loss or a signed-out user, and the service route needs a dedicated account with file-mode keys, since LocalSystem cannot decrypt the user's DPAPI key.
- Why it matters: A Windows or Mac storage node is down after each unattended reboot until someone logs in.
- Proposed change: Scope Goal 1 and Principle 7: no privileges for the client role and for storage on Linux; unattended storage on macOS or Windows takes one admin step, a LaunchDaemon or service under a dedicated account, or relies on auto-login; the status page shows login-scoped storage nodes.

### F33. macOS TCC and Full Disk Access are never mentioned
- Where: Backup, Scan; Packaging; Risks.
- The RFC says: "without them users click through a warning once".
- What is wrong: Reading ~/Desktop, ~/Documents or Mail data needs a TCC grant bound to the executable's code identity; a denied prompt yields EPERM, the scan specifies no error handling, so a run can end complete with folders missing. Ad-hoc signed arm64 binaries lose grants on every in-place update.
- Why it matters: A Mac member believes Documents are backed up when they are not.
- Proposed change: A per-folder access probe in the wizard and Scan, run inside the launchd agent; a counted "skipped for permission errors" state that marks the run incomplete; a stable Developer ID tied to self-update; a macOS risk row; the macOS 15 flow in place of the quoted sentence.

### F34. The storage disk at runtime is unspecified
- Where: Native engine, Share on disk; Health, Census; F9.
- The RFC says: "the October 2026 incident must be impossible".
- What is wrong: No marker check is described beyond the empty-folder prompt: if an external disk disappears while running, the index keeps reporting shares the server cannot serve, and on macOS any MkdirAll recreates the ejected /Volumes path on the boot volume, so PutShare writes onto the system disk, the incident F9 names.
- Why it matters: F9's promise is not kept by the design that cites it.
- Proposed change: Re-verify the marker before every storage operation and census tick and on volume events; failure enters a storage-unavailable state, UNAVAILABLE rather than NOT_FOUND, withholds the census and shows "disk missing"; repair treats it as down, never lost; record the directory with volume identity.

### F35. UDP-only transport and relay, and IPv6 absent
- Where: Transport; Hub, Two hubs; Risks.
- The RFC says: "The relay needs a public UDP port".
- What is wrong: Networks that block outbound UDP leave a member with no path at all; tinc falls back to TCP 655 today, so guest, campus and hotel networks that allow arbitrary TCP regress. The iroh branch of the spike would cover it by accident; the Go branch has nothing. IPv6 appears nowhere.
- Why it matters: Laptop members on office networks, the people the rewrite is for, see "no peers".
- Proposed change: Decide a relay fallback over TLS on TCP 443 carrying the end-to-end QUIC packets, or declare UDP-blocked networks unsupported with a risk row and a client-side diagnosis; name hubs by hostname with A and AAAA records; add both cases to the spike.

### F36. Relay-to-direct switching is a layer the plan never sizes
- Where: Transport, Connecting; Language table; Spike plan; NFR Transfers.
- The RFC says: "a thin relay to write (a few hundred lines)".
- What is wrong: QUIC migration covers the client's own address change; moving a flow from relay to direct happens beneath QUIC in iroh's and Tailscale's magicsock, thousands of lines of virtual addresses, authenticated probes and relay re-binding. The Transfers NFR demands that a switch keeps the transfer, and the spike does not measure it. Two lenses raised it.
- Why it matters: The language decision and the phase 1 calendar rest on a cost off by an order of magnitude.
- Proposed change: Either keep the NFR, reprice the Go transport as quic-go plus pion plus an owned path-selection layer against iroh, and make mid-transfer switching a G0 exit criterion; or relax the NFR to a resume within seconds after a path change.

### F37. 80 MB/s of AES-256-GCM on a Pi 4 is not credible on one core
- Where: NFR Throughput; Native engine, Encryption; Spike plan.
- The RFC says: "above 80 MB/s on a Pi 4".
- What is wrong: The BCM2711 has no ARMv8 crypto extensions, so Go's AES-GCM runs generic code at tens of MB/s per core; four cores can plausibly reach the figure, but the RFC does not say so and the spike measures Reed-Solomon only. The materiality skeptic dissented, since the Pi stays uplink-bound on the live network.
- Why it matters: Local-peer restore and repair are bounded by software AES.
- Proposed change: Add AES-256-GCM, ChaCha20-Poly1305 and BLAKE3 throughput on the Pi 4, single- and multi-core, to the spike. Pick ChaCha20-Poly1305, or name the AEAD in the existing header versioning and restate the figure as four-core.

### F40. The fork kit has no design section
- Where: F23; Principle 9; Self-update; Success metrics; Phases.
- The RFC says: "a second network has run for a month on an unmodified binary".
- What is wrong: GitHub is named but never stated as a requirement for forks. Self-update reads "the signed release index" without saying whose: following the original is a downstream dependency to state; running its own needs a release key. The wizard has no network-selection step, and the metric expects a trial community after phase 1 while Phases validates the guide in phase 4.
- Why it matters: A forker cannot tell what they need, and the phase 1 metric has no path to measurement.
- Proposed change: Decide the fork's update channel and where the release key lives, with the consequence for Principle 9; state that GitHub is assumed and what a fork needs; add a network descriptor in an invite link; move the metric or the validation.

### F42. Principle 5 promises stated rules; none are stated
- Where: Principle 5; Users, Forker row; Security model.
- The RFC says: "the rules that rest on that trust are stated".
- What is wrong: No section says what members may store, who may invoke removal and on what grounds, what a host discloses under a legal demand, or when a silent or deceased member is marked leaving; the mechanism exists, the policy does not. The materiality skeptic rated it minor.
- Why it matters: Operators have no agreed basis for the one remedy they hold; forkers inherit the gap.
- Proposed change: A "Community rules" section or a fork-kit COMMUNITY.md template: what members agree to store, who may invoke leaving and removal, a dormant-member trigger, a legal-demand position; plus a threat row for a legal demand to a host. Or narrow Principle 5 to its own test.

### G01. The local page has no CSRF, Origin or Host defence
- Where: Local API, CLI and UI; Security model.
- The RFC says: "`open` (prints a one-time link that logs the browser into the local page)".
- What is wrong: The session mechanism is unspecified, and the only one described, a logged-in browser posting forms to 127.0.0.1, is CSRF-vulnerable: no browser blocks a top-level form post to loopback, and nothing binds state changes to a credential a cross-site page cannot produce or checks Host and Origin. Syncthing, the named source, has all three guards.
- Why it matters: A visited page can certify an attacker's device with the member signing key or change retention so cleanup deletes snapshots.
- Proposed change: Every state change carries the bearer token or a per-session CSRF token; HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict cookies; a Host allowlist; Origin checked on posts; GET never mutates. Add adversary rows for a visited web page and another local process or user.

### G02. The LAN binding is cleartext HTTP carrying full authority
- Where: Local API; Service integration; NFR Privileges and Observability.
- The RFC says: "Binding to the LAN is an opt-in for a headless Pi managed from a laptop."
- What is wrong: No TLS, password or endpoint scope is specified for a non-loopback binding, so the token, the login link, settings and the wizard cross the home Wi-Fi in cleartext with every loopback endpoint, certify and update included. Nothing says how `redundanet open` over SSH reads the token under the Pi image's dedicated user.
- Why it matters: Token capture on the LAN equals full member authority, because the signing key is on the device.
- Proposed change: Simplest: drop LAN binding and state SSH port-forwarding plus the CLI as the headless path, which matches live practice. Otherwise TLS pinned through the `open` link, a pairing step for the phone, and a read-only LAN scope with certify, update, settings and the wizard on loopback only.

### G03. The recovery phrase is shown and typed in a browser page, monthly
- Where: Identity, Losing the phrase; User journeys; Backup, Restore test; Local API.
- The RFC says: "The derivation starts from the phrase the member types, not from stored keys".
- What is wrong: The seed is the one secret with no revocation, and nothing restricts which binding may show or collect it; the RFC's own Pi story, managed from a laptop and usable from a phone, routes the twelve words over cleartext HTTP, against the network-path row, and the scheduled restore test multiplies the exposure.
- Why it matters: A LAN sniffer or a cached page obtains the member's whole identity.
- Proposed change: Phrase prompts only on loopback or through the later pairing flow; the automatic restore test uses stored keys and "verify my paper copy" is an explicit action at most quarterly. See F22.

### G05. The join endpoint's HTTP surface is unspecified
- Where: Hub, Join endpoint; Membership, Joining; Security model.
- The RFC says: "checks that the named inviter is an active member, rate-limits, and opens the pull request with a bot token".
- What is wrong: A join request comes from a self-minted key, so "rate-limits" names no key the requester can be held to, and every accepted request spends the bot token and a CI run. The hub entry has no join URL, the VPS hub has no HTTPS story, and "the only public surface" omits the join endpoint.
- Why it matters: An unauthenticated public POST spends the hub's only write path.
- Proposed change: A precondition that costs the requester something before any PR, such as an inviter-issued code; limiter keys of source IP and member key with one pending request per key; what the bot credential is and what its suspension does; `join:` in the hub entry. See F12.

### G09. The key comes from the scan's read, the ciphertext from a later one
- Where: Backup, Scan and Upload; Native engine, Key and storage index.
- The RFC says: "A miss hashes the file with BLAKE3, derives the convergent key and storage index".
- What is wrong: Upload encrypts from a second read that may be days later; if the file changed, the object sits under content 1's storage index with content 2's ciphertext, every check passes, and every later file with content 1 dedups to it and restores as content 2. The spool-and-rewind alternative is never budgeted.
- Why it matters: Silent wrong bytes at restore with no error anywhere, from the native engine onward.
- Proposed change: State in Upload that the bytes hashed for the key are the bytes encrypted: hash the second read while encrypting and re-scan on mismatch. At Restore and in the restore test require BLAKE3_keyed(secret, plaintext_hash) to equal the cap's key.

### G10. Change detection drops ctime
- Where: Backup, Scan and Restore test; F13; F17.
- The RFC says: "look up (path, size, mtime, file id) in the device's local database".
- What is wrong: Tahoe's backupdb, which every live node uses, compares ctime as well; without it an in-place write that leaves size and mtime unchanged, VeraCrypt containers by default, files held open and updated in place, is never backed up again. File id is undefined per platform and no hit is ever re-hashed.
- Why it matters: A class of real files is frozen in every snapshot forever under a green restore test.
- Proposed change: Add ctime on Unix and ChangeTime on Windows to the scan key; define file id per platform with a hash fallback; compare the restore test against the live file when it exists; a periodic bounded re-hash for the held-open case.

### G11. No restorable snapshot until the whole queue drains
- Where: Backup, Publish and Retention; journey "Backup"; Users table.
- The RFC says: "A backup that is interrupted leaves no visible snapshot".
- What is wrong: The first restore point arrives after the entire initial set is uploaded, two days by the RFC's own journey and longer on a sleeping laptop, while the Users table promises "first backup the same evening". A first backup longer than seven days also sees its day-one objects, referenced by no published root, become eligible for another device's cleanup.
- Why it matters: The member with the slowest uplink has no restore point for days.
- Proposed change: Publish a partial snapshot at the end of every run, naming the subtrees fully covered and the scan start as its time; say how retention counts partial snapshots; rewrite the Users table to "first restorable snapshot within the first run".

### G13. Scan's HasShares path is not in the Engine interface
- Where: Backup, Scan; Storage engine interface; F17.
- The RFC says: "the backup job is already engine-neutral in phase 1".
- What is wrong: Scan asks `HasShares` by storage index before uploading, but the interface has only Check and Repair by Cap, and the adapter over Tahoe's web API has no presence query without a cap, so in phase 1 a miss is a full `PUT /uri`, read and encoded locally before Tahoe skips the transfer.
- Why it matters: The engine swap touches the job the RFC says it does not touch.
- Proposed change: Add `Has(ctx, plaintextHash, size) (Cap, bool, error)` to the interface, native via HasShares plus a descriptor fetch, unsupported on the adapter; key the local database by content hash and by engine; reword Scan so the cheap cross-device path is phase 2.

### G14. The cache hit never revalidates the capability
- Where: Backup, Scan; Health, Rebalance; Migration plan, step 4.
- The RFC says: "a hit reuses the recorded capability without reading the file".
- What is wrong: Rebalance re-encodes and re-publishes, but nothing updates or invalidates the device's local database, so the next run rebuilds directories from the cache and references the old-encoding object again; the live network hit exactly this and added prune_stale. Step 4 has the same shape with Tahoe caps in native snapshots.
- Why it matters: F19's conversion reaches 100% and regresses at the next backup run.
- Proposed change: A cache contract: a row whose capability's engine or encoding differs from the current one is a miss; rebalance updates every row it re-encodes before re-publishing; the engine switch marks every row stale. See F18.

### G17. Restore write semantics are missing
- Where: Backup, Restore and Scan; F15.
- The RFC says: "decrypted and written with its mtime".
- What is wrong: A restore into a folder the 15-minute job scans has each half-written file picked up as a miss and uploaded as a garbage object, and those snapshots can survive retention as a day's keeper; no temp-name-and-rename, no pause of the set, no free-space precheck, and mode is not said to be restored. The materiality skeptic dissented.
- Why it matters: A restore after a mistaken delete produces garbage objects and an undefined mix on a full disk.
- Proposed change: Under Restore: temporary name and rename on completion; pause the backup set for the restored subtree; precheck free space; apply mode after data and directory mtimes after children; state that ownership is not restored.

### G19. Repair and deletion fight with no tombstone
- Where: Health, Repair and Deletion; Ownership; Engine interface.
- The RFC says: "deletes the rest with a signed token".
- What is wrong: A server honouring a delete token simply forgets the object; when one holder was offline, the placement table shows it under-placed, a worker regenerates it, PutShare passes every listed check, and it is back at n of n and charged to the owner. The cleanup can re-issue tokens only for objects it can enumerate.
- Why it matters: Pruned snapshots and deleted files never leave the grid, and F14 is false in phase 2.
- Proposed change: Servers keep a tombstone per owner and storage index for grace_days, refuse a repair-worker PutShare for it, let an owner-device PutShare clear it, and report tombstones in the census; the cleanup gets a grid-wide listing of its storage indices from the hub. See F15 and G27.

### G20. The 15% reserve cannot absorb the loss of the largest server at budget
- Where: Ownership, The budget; Health, Repair; NFR Durability.
- The RFC says: "kept for repair during outages and for snapshots that overlap during pruning".
- What is wrong: At budget a survivor has exactly the reserve free while a dead server at budget held 85% of its contribution, so full repair onto survivors needs reserve_percent at least the largest server's share of the grid; nothing ties the reserve to the manifest. Objects stay readable at k, so Principle 2 holds but the Durability row does not.
- Why it matters: After the first large loss at budget the page shows "repair running" forever and nobody is told to add disk.
- Proposed change: `redundanet validate` refuses or warns on reserve_percent below the largest storage node's share of total contribution; repair writes bypass owner budget and per-server cap but not the physical floor; a "repair blocked: X GB short" state on the page.

### G22. Fewer storage servers than n is unspecified
- Where: Backup, Upload; NFR Availability; Open questions; journey "I start my own community".
- The RFC says: "written to what is there, recorded as under-placed, and left to repair".
- What is wrong: The RFC never says how many shares a server takes when S is below n: under the natural reading a one- or two-member fork shows 100% under-placed forever with a repair queue no worker can drain; under one-share-per-server its objects are unreadable. Validation does not check k and n against the storage list.
- Why it matters: The forker journey yields a network in permanent repair on the guide's first afternoon.
- Proposed change: Place min(n, S) shares on distinct servers and the rest doubled up, never fewer than k distinct; validation refuses k above S and warns when n exceeds S; the collector's target is min(n, S_live), as today's monitor does; derive the default k-of-n from the storage count.

### G24. The bandwidth cap is silent on serving and repair traffic
- Where: Transport, Bandwidth; F8; NFR Throughput and Durability; Success metrics.
- The RFC says: "The limit applies to storage traffic, never to control messages".
- What is wrong: Read plainly, the cap also throttles serving shares to other members' restores and repair workers, so every restore is paced by the tightest cap among holders; read the other way, F8 fails because serving saturates the uplink. Regenerating a lost 1 TB node moves about 2.5 TB of home uplink, outside the 7-day metric.
- Why it matters: Either every restore is paced by the most restrictive holder or F8 is false.
- Proposed change: Two traffic classes per direction, own and service, each with a bucket and a stated priority; F8 applies to the own class. Align the Throughput row with the Durability row and restate the 7-day metric as repair throughput per day.

### G26. Rebalance runs inside the budget that migration exempts
- Where: Health, Rebalance; Ownership; Migration plan, step 4; F19.
- The RFC says: "which they can meet because the Tahoe shares still count only against the old grid".
- What is wrong: Re-encoded shares are charged to the owner and the old object is freed only when every snapshot naming it is re-published, so the change needs free budget equal to the member's usage and stalls above 50%; migration got an exemption, native rebalance none. F19's "without human action" also conflicts with Health. The domain skeptic dissented.
- Why it matters: A k-of-n change leaves every member near budget half-converted with both encodings charged.
- Proposed change: The manifest declares a transition {from, to, deadline}; during it servers account old-encoding shares at zero or raise each budget by the old-encoding usage, and cleanup deletes per converted snapshot. F19 says rebalance needs the owner's devices.

## Minor

### F28. No rule for members who do not migrate
Where: Migration plan, steps 4 and 5; G3. The RFC says: "When every member has, operators stop the Tahoe servers". What is wrong: no notice, deadline or fate for a straggler's shares, and no over-quota rule at cutover. Why it matters: phase 3 cannot end without an unbounded wait. Proposed change: a straggler rule, an over-quota rule using the reserve or sponsor levers, and G3 with a bounded wait.

### F29. "From the pinned lock file" is false for Tahoe
Where: Tahoe adapter; Packaging. The RFC says: "from the pinned lock file, as the Docker images are today". What is wrong: Tahoe and its tree are not in poetry.lock; pip resolves them afresh, and the PyInstaller job would resolve on every release build. Why it matters: the pyOpenSSL scar can recur; rollback is unaffected. Proposed change: a hash-pinned requirements file used by both builds; reword both sentences to promise it.

### F38. Phase 1 holds nearly the whole product in three months
Where: Phases; Success metrics. The RFC says: "Phase 1, the agent on Tahoe (3 months)". What is wrong: identity, mesh, bridge, bundle, census, backup job, local page, three service integrations, self-update, hub mode and nine node migrations, with no estimate basis; the fork trial is validated in phase 4 while the metric expects it after phase 1. Why it matters: the February 2027 date is unsupported. Proposed change: name migration steps 1 to 3 as gates with estimates, record the hours-per-week basis, move the fork-trial metric or the validation.

### F39. G1 couples the first cross-platform member to every node's reinstall
Where: Spike plan, gates; Phases; Migration plan, steps 2 and 3. The RFC says: "This is the phase 1 exit test." against "tinc is gone from every node". What is wrong: step 2 is labelled the exit test but tinc disappears only in step 3, which has no phase label, duration or gate and depends on five other members' hosts. Four lenses raised it. Why it matters: the first gate cannot be passed by the step named for it. Proposed change: G1a, a macOS or Windows member stores and restores through the bridge; G1b, tinc gone everywhere; phase 2 starts at G1a.

### F41. The risk table and the packaging section disagree on signing
Where: Risks; Packaging. The RFC says: "Signed releases (Authenticode) from the start" against "a separate decision with a cost". What is wrong: neither names the recurring cost or the hardware-held Authenticode key; "click through a warning once" is imprecise on macOS 15; no row covers this project's PyInstaller bundle on Windows and macOS arm64. Why it matters: G1's first run is described wrongly. Proposed change: decide signing at G0 with a budget line and CI mechanism; describe the macOS 15 flow; add a bundle check on both platforms to the spike.

### F43. Delete-token expiry and network binding are unspecified
Where: Identity; Mutable state; Health, Deletion. The RFC says: "`sign(member, storage index, "delete", expiry)`". What is wrong: storage indices are convergent, so a captured token is valid against a re-uploaded copy until an expiry the RFC never bounds; no signed record names the network, so a host in two forks can replay across them. Why it matters: replayed deletes, avoidable with a few bytes per record. Proposed change: expiry below 7 days or bind the token to the descriptor hash; every signed record carries the network name.

### F44. Time-based rules never say whose clock
Where: Backup, Retention; Health, Deletion; Mutable state. The RFC says: "objects younger than 7 days are never deleted". What is wrong: whether the server judges age by the owner-asserted `created` or its own store time is unstated; the root `version` is not defined as a counter; Pis boot without an RTC. Why it matters: a skewed device can lose the guard for another device's objects. Proposed change: one "clocks" paragraph: the guard uses the server's store time; version is a persisted counter recovered as max(GetRoots)+1; certificates carry no validity window.

### F48. The thin transport gathers STUN candidates only
Where: Transport, Connecting; Spike plan. The RFC says: "the address candidates it learns from STUN-style reflection". What is wrong: the hand-built version gathers no host, IPv6 or port-mapped candidates, so two devices of one member on one LAN hairpin through the relay, and the spike tests no same-LAN or IPv6 pair. Why it matters: the hairpin case is not caught before the transport decision. Proposed change: enumerate candidate types, add port mapping and IPv6 best-effort, and add a same-LAN pair and an IPv6 pair to the spike.

### F50. The loopback bridge's port model is under-specified
Where: Transport, The loopback bridge; Migration plan, step 1. The RFC says: "`tub.location = tcp:127.0.0.1:<its own port>`". What is wrong: a manifest-assigned port must be free on every member's machine; today's writer omits interface=, so Tahoe binds all interfaces; force_foolscap = true is unstated; bridge traffic starts at step 1 whenever its negotiation wins. Why it matters: phase 1 laptops expose their tub. Proposed change: specify `tub.port = tcp:<port>:interface=127.0.0.1`, a loopback web.port and force_foolscap = true; state the hint race in step 1.

### F51. Phase 1 introducer plumbing is unspecified
Where: Membership; Transport; Hub, Phase 1 extra. The RFC says: "phase 1 keeps them in a side file". What is wrong: a native member in step 2 cannot learn the introducer FURL or the hub's loopback port, since v3 hub entries carry neither; the fate of the hub-fly introducer is undecided although hub-hetzner already runs a second one. Why it matters: a native agent cannot write introducer.furl. Proposed change: phase-1-only hub fields introducer_furl and port served by the directory role; step 1 rewrites each FURL to the two-hint form; decide the fly introducer's fate.

### F52. Step 3 silently retires SFTP, the mount and backup_sync
Where: Migration plan, step 3; Non-goals. The RFC says: "removes the compose stack". What is wrong: the stack is also the SFTP frontend, the FUSE mount in the quickstart and backup_sync of SYNC_DIR; the RFC never says they go or what replaces them. Why it matters: documented member features vanish without notice. Proposed change: one line in step 3 and the non-goals: SFTP and the mount are retired; SYNC_DIR becomes the agent's backup job.

### F53. The sidecar has two update paths and no health check
Where: Self-update; Migration plan, step 1. The RFC says: "A failed check swaps the previous binary back". What is wrong: in-container self-update writes a filesystem the next recreate discards; a sidecar that runs but cannot reach the hub or bind the bridge passes today's `redundanet update` check, so auto-rollback never fires. Why it matters: a broken sidecar takes native members' only path to that server down. Proposed change: the sidecar build disables self-update and gets a compose healthcheck, hub reached and bridge listening; F20 applies to native installs only.

### F54. Two grids on one disk with separate accounting
Where: Migration plan, step 4; Ownership, Free space. The RFC says: "the Tahoe shares still count only against the old grid". What is wrong: separate accounting creates no physical room; on a shared disk the native quota a server can honour is free space minus Tahoe's reservation, and the native floor is undefined. Why it matters: one full disk under two healthy quotas stalls migration there. Proposed change: define the native floor, say at step 4 that capacity is physical and shared, and flag a server whose free space is below the native contribution it backs.

### F55. Logs exist only as an in-memory ring buffer
Where: NFR Observability and Supportability; Local API. The RFC says: "Structured logs with a ring buffer visible in the local page". What is wrong: no other sink is specified and the bundle is an API call, so a service that panics at start under launchd or Task Scheduler leaves nothing a member can send. Why it matters: the first non-Linux support case cannot be diagnosed. Proposed change: rotating log files in the data directory, a crash handler, the OS sink for start failures, and `redundanet diagnostics` working from files when the agent is down.

### F56. Directory entry names have no encoding or platform mapping
Where: Mutable state, Directory objects; Backup, Restore. The RFC says: "name, type, size, mtime, mode". What is wrong: Linux names are bytes, NTFS and APFS are case-insensitive, APFS normalizes, Windows rejects reserved names; a same-restore collision is dropped, overwritten or misnamed depending on the existing-file policy, invisibly to the restore test. Why it matters: cross-platform restores lose files silently. Proposed change: define name as a byte string with a UTF-8 flag; detect collisions before writing; map invalid names deterministically with a report; count every skipped or renamed entry against "complete". See G16.

### F57. "iroh is young" is out of date
Where: Risks; Language table; Spike plan. The RFC says: "iroh is young; libp2p is heavy". What is wrong: iroh reached 1.0 on 15 June 2026 and is at 1.4.1 with a self-hosted relay; its Go binding is community-maintained and needs cgo, which the static-binary rationale excludes. Why it matters: the fallback is weighed against a stale picture at G0. Proposed change: update the row to "1.x since June 2026", measure an iroh endpoint in the spike beside quic-go plus pion, and list the cgo binding as rejected.

### F58. "Python has no userspace NAT-traversal library" is overstated
Where: Alternatives considered. The RFC says: "Python has no userspace NAT-traversal library". What is wrong: aioice implements ICE, aioquic exists, and iroh ships Python bindings over a Rust core; the defensible claim is that Python has no integrated QUIC, hole-punching and relay stack of iroh's or quic-go's maturity. Why it matters: a reviewer who knows aiortc trusts the rest of the table less. Proposed change: rewrite the clause that way, or rest the row on bundle size, startup, antivirus friction and the per-platform runtime.

### F59. The MinIO and SeaweedFS row is wrong on erasure coding
Where: Alternatives considered. The RFC says: "replication rather than erasure coding across homes". What is wrong: MinIO's core mode is Reed-Solomon erasure sets, from the author of the library the RFC picks; SeaweedFS offers Reed-Solomon too; only Garage replicates. Why it matters: a factual error beside the library choice it contradicts. Proposed change: "designed for one trusted cluster with server-held keys; Garage replicates, MinIO and SeaweedFS erasure-code within a cluster, none accounts per owner across untrusted homes".

### F60. The restic chunking credit is not in the design
Where: Alternatives; Backup, Chunking; F13. The RFC says: "The chunking and repository-index ideas behind the backup job". What is wrong: dedup is per whole file with fixed 64 MiB chunks for resumability, not content-defined; a 2 GB file touched daily costs about 18 hours of upload at 0.5 Mbit/s and 2-of-4. Why it matters: "incrementally" promises more than it delivers. Proposed change: state the per-file dedup decision in Backup and restore and F13, credit restic for the repository index and snapshot model, and reserve a content-defined chunk kind for a later format version.

### G04. The public status page's projection is undefined
Where: Health, What the status page can now say; Hub; NFR Privacy; F18. The RFC says: "The hub learns liveness and census summaries". What is wrong: the collector holds storage indices, owners and per-device liveness; the page adds per named member "last backup" and "last verified restore" with no granularity or opt-in; today's aggregates-only rule survives only as "shape and honesty rules"; the Privacy row and the Hub table disagree. Why it matters: forks inherit an undefined privacy posture. Proposed change: a "Public projection" paragraph listing what status.json carries, what stays on the hub, and what appears only locally or at day granularity; reword F18 to counts per copies-class.

### G06. The diagnostics bundle has no defined contents or recipient
Where: NFR Supportability; Local API. The RFC says: "No secrets in it". What is wrong: "last errors" from the backup job will carry absolute paths and the home-directory username, which Principle 1 says the maintainer never sees; "secret" is undefined and no destination is named. Why it matters: the first support ticket leaks file names. Proposed change: an allowlist with paths replaced by the backup set's label plus a hash, a file the member sends by hand, and file and folder names declared secrets.

### G12. Cloud placeholder files are never mentioned
Where: Backup, Scan; journey "Backup"; NFR Platforms. The RFC says: "A miss hashes the file with BLAKE3". What is wrong: a default Windows 11 or macOS install keeps Desktop and Documents under OneDrive Files On-Demand or iCloud Drive; the first backup, each new device and each database rebuild hydrate every evicted file onto disk and into the grid against quota. Why it matters: the first Mac or Windows member fills their laptop and allocation with cloud-only files. Proposed change: one sentence under Scan, placeholders skipped by default and counted, with a per-set opt-in; a per-platform default exclude list; cloud placeholders in the risk rows.

### G15. Locked files on Windows have no stated behaviour, and "mode" is undefined there
Where: Backup, Scan and Restore; Mutable state; Principle 7; Risks. The RFC says: "paths and locks differ". What is wrong: VSS needs administrator rights, which Principle 7 forbids, so exclusively held files fail at open with no per-file contract, which today's entrypoint has; "mode" has no meaning on Windows and no restore rule per target. Why it matters: either the run fails on the first locked file or files drop silently. Proposed change: a per-file failure contract under Scan: skip, count, list, retry next run, never fail the snapshot; mode as POSIX bits with a read-only mapping on Windows; VSS out of scope. See G16.

### G16. File kinds are unspecified
Where: Mutable state, Directory objects; Backup, Scan and Restore. The RFC says: "name, type, size, mtime, mode". What is wrong: the values of "type" are never listed; symlinks and special files, which `tahoe backup` skips with a warning today, have no stated behaviour, so a symlink loop is an unbounded walk; hardlinks, sparse files, xattrs and reparse points are undefined. Why it matters: the contract for a restored home directory does not exist. Proposed change: a table under Scan: symlinks stored as links and never followed, hardlinks as copies, special files and reparse points skipped and counted, the rest out of scope under the versioned entry format. See G15 and F56.

### G21. The budget formula ignores where shares can go
Where: Ownership; Backup, Upload; F10; F11. The RFC says: "placement skips such servers". What is wrong: nothing says what happens when the skip leaves fewer than n servers with room; with S=8 and headroom 2 the per-server cap equals budget/4 and never binds at n=4, so only the free-space floor protects a 250 GB disk and whoever backs up first owns it. Why it matters: small servers fill first-come-first-served under enforced quotas. Proposed change: define the short-placement condition as fewer than n eligible servers and say whether the object is under-placed or the backup pauses; bound the per-server cap by the server's own contribution. See F16.

### G23. Allocation drops today's reduction to the real disk
Where: Membership, Allocation; Ownership, Free space; Principle 8. The RFC says: "Unchanged in formula" and "as it does today". What is wrong: today compute_quotas allocates from min(claimed, census disk total); the RFC keeps only the flag, so a 1 TB declaration on a 61.6 GB disk, a case the live network has had, would get 850 GB of enforced budget on everyone else's disks. Why it matters: fairness rests on a reviewer reading a number. Proposed change: say the v3 input is the declared contribution as reviewed in the PR, drop the two phrases, and note that a census-based clamp, which can only shrink a budget, may come later.

### G25. "Rebuilt from any k shares" never says which k
Where: Backup, Restore; Health, Repair; NFR Throughput. The RFC says: "each file is rebuilt from any k shares". What is wrong: no holder selection or hedging to the spare shares is specified, so a read that picks the 0.5 Mbit/s holder runs that share at about 62 KB/s; Tahoe does the same, so this is parity, but the design can fix it cheaply. Why it matters: restores depend on luck. Proposed change: fetch blocks from all n holders per segment, keep the first k, cancel the rest; prefer holders with the best recent throughput; qualify the Throughput row to the k fastest holders.

### G27. The repair trigger has no quiescence window
Where: Health, Census and Repair; Backup, Chunking. The RFC says: "turns under-placed objects into tasks". What is wrong: an object whose first shares completed is under-placed for the rest of its upload, 36 minutes per chunk at 0.5 Mbit/s, and the collector has no age or in-progress threshold, so a slow first backup is scheduled for repair while the client is still sending; a second writer on a partial share is undefined. Why it matters: duplicate work for every object of a slow first backup. Proposed change: create a task only after a quiescence window since the last PutShare touched the object; say whether a partial share counts as present and how a second writer is handled. See F17 and F21.

## Checked and dismissed

- F45, whole-share deletion on one bad block and the unpinned Reed-Solomon matrix: the layout sentence describes disk order, not wire order; a bad share dropped at 2-of-4 leaves k readable and a repair task within the hour; klauspost's default matrix is stable across v1 and the descriptor carries version, k and n.
- F46, reflection and spoofed path switching: the RFC names the probe-then-switch model and defers wire details to the spike; RFC 9000 path validation prevents off-path steering, and the reflection vector is a few 1200-byte Initials with no amplification.
- F47, NAT timeouts and firewalls against "cached direct path": every direct path is a simultaneous open from the socket holding the outbound hub connection, so non-admin inbound rules do not touch it, and retries refresh cone-NAT mappings; the symmetric case is already hedged.
- F49, the loopback bridge as a local privilege boundary: same-user processes already hold the agent's token and the member's source files; multi-user hosts are not in the live network; the mesh allowlist refuses non-members before any application byte.
- G07, `/settings` exposing hubs and the manifest URL: the hub is defined as untrusted, so adding one gains only what a compromised listed hub has; the same token already reaches certify and restore; the manifest URL is unavoidably local configuration.
- G08, CLI behaviour with the agent down and API versioning across the swap: `service` commands sit outside the API-only group, `/status` reports versions, and the skew window is the seconds between swap and restart.
- G18, the polling scan against a 1M-file design point: the million is grid-wide shares on a storage node, not files on one laptop; real-time sync is a stated non-goal; the rebuild sentence already describes the two-tier seed.
